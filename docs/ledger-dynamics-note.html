Ledger dynamics over the claim algebra

A formal note registering the development ledger's conventions against the calculus: the dictionary, one new operation with its hazard, one new sort, and three process axioms. Every lemma, proposition, and theorem below is machine-checked (validate_note.py, 22 checks, all passing); each result cites its check. Definitions here are numbered L-series to avoid collision with the base documents. 2026-07-13.

0. Scope and method

This note extends claim-calculus.html and claim-algebra.html; it changes nothing in them. Part 1 is a dictionary: ledger conventions that turn out to be existing algebra, cited by definition number. Parts 2–4 are new structure: the truth-join at testimony level (with the laundering hazard it carries and the sound certification forms), the interval-testimony sort the empirical courts use, and the process axioms (commitment and the restless corners) with consistency, independence, and conservativity established. Everything provable was proved twice: once on paper below, once by machine against a faithful implementation of Definitions 2.4–2.16 and 6.5; the appendix lists the checks. One definition in this note was refuted by its own validation before reaching its final form, and the refutation is kept as a remark, per the house rule that corrections are content.

2. Existence composition — the truth-join, its hazard, and the sound forms

Definition L1 (propositional slot). A propositional slot is a slot whose candidate set is the singleton {⊤}; its testimonies are determined by the one graded pair (pro, con). Claims of the form "an event of kind K occurred in habitat h" are carried on propositional slots, one per habitat.
Definition L2 (truth-join, ∨ₜ, propositional). For propositional testimonies a, b, the truth-join is channelwise: (a ∨ₜ b)(⊤) = ( pro(a)+pro(b), con(a)·con(b) ), normalized. Support pools by alternative; refutation must be joint — the disjunction is only told-against when every disjunct is.

Remark L3 (a definition refuted by its own check). The first candidate definition, the De Morgan dual ¬∧ₜ(¬a,¬b) over the calculus's candidate-pairing conjunctions, is wrong: the gap ε has no candidates to pair, so every pairing operation annihilates on it, and T ∨ₜ N computed to N. The machine caught it (four checks failed) before registration. Disjunction is a join, not a pairing; Definition L2 is channelwise for exactly this reason, and its scope is propositional slots; a general-candidate disjunction is left open. [V4, first-run failure preserved in the script's comment]

Proposition L4 (corner table). Under Definition L2 the sixteen corner compositions match the Belnap truth-join: T dominates; F ∨ₜ F = F; N ∨ₜ F = N; B ∨ₜ F = B; N ∨ₜ B = T.
Proof. Channel arithmetic per cell: root-pro is zero iff both pros are zero (sum in ℕ[X] is zero-sum-free, Def 2.2); root-con is zero iff either con is zero (a product with a zero factor is zero, and ℕ[X] has no zero divisors for the converse). The β map of Def 2.6 then gives each cell. Machine-checked over all sixteen. [V4: ∨ₜ 16-cell table] ∎
Theorem L5 (dual laundering). ∨ₜ launders refutation through unchecked habitats: if any leaf has an empty con-channel, the root's con is zero regardless of contests elsewhere. In particular corner(N ∨ₜ B) = T, and the gate of Def 2.15 signs a purely contested existence when one habitat is merely unchecked.
Proof. Root-con is the product of leaf cons (Def L2); one empty factor zeroes it. The B leaf contributes its pro to the root's sum, so root-pro is nonzero: corner T by Def 2.6, and with a singleton candidate, card = 1; the gate's remaining conjuncts are grade and verify, neither of which sees the vanished con. This is the third named strain of the laundering genus: ⊗ₖ erases contests through its identity B; verify = C ∨ O signed through its weak branch; ∨ₜ erases contests through any con-empty leaf. Same genus, new vector. [V4: dual laundering exhibit; naive root signs while no leaf signs] ∎
Theorem L6 (sound existence certification and refutation). For an existence claim over registered habitats h₁…hₙ carried on propositional slots: (confirm) certify the existence iff some leaf itself passes the gate (the witness rule); never gate the ∨ₜ root. (refute) the root reads F iff every leaf reads F; an N leaf blocks refutation and never enables confirmation.
Proof. Confirmation: a signing leaf has corner T, clean con, grade ≥ θ, verified (Def 2.15): a witnessed occurrence; the root then also reads T (Prop L4, T-dominance), but by Theorem L5 the converse fails, so the certification predicate must be the leaf's gate, not the root's. Refutation: root-con nonzero requires every factor nonzero, and root-pro zero requires every summand zero; jointly, every leaf is (0, con≠0) = F. An N leaf has empty con (blocking the product) and empty pro (contributing nothing), giving both blocking claims. This mirrors, on the join side, the correction doc's two constructions on the meet side: per-slot gating certifies; the bare composed corner does not. [V4: witness sound; totality; N blocks F] ∎
Corollary L7 (the P4 branch rules are L6). With habitats {typed events, prose annotations, wire-dropped grammar}: Branch A (all habitats checked clean) is L6's refutation totality: refuted-by-exhaustion is the all-F root. Branches B and C (an attempt found in a habitat) present a candidate witness leaf for the operator's gate: the witness rule with the human verifier as verify. The pre-grep state (typed F, wire habitat N) could not refute, which is why the instructions demanded the grep before any verdict. ∎

3. Interval testimony — the empirical courts as a sort

Definition L8 (interval testimony). An interval testimony for an estimand with registered margin δ is ι = (lo, hi, δ, ceiling?) with lo ≤ hi: the decision interval a registered procedure computed, plus the pre-registered flag for a range-compressed reading.
Definition L9 (interval corner). corner_ι(ι) is: U_ceiling if the ceiling flag is set; else U_width if (hi−lo)/2 > δ; else T_dir if lo > δ; else F_dir if hi < −δ; else Flat.
Lemma L10 (exit partition). Exactly one exit fires for every interval.
Proof. The clauses are ordered and the last three are mutually exclusive under the width bound: half-width ≤ δ makes lo > δ and hi < −δ incompatible, and Flat is the stated remainder. Machine-checked over 900+ grid intervals. [V5: total & deterministic] ∎
Definition L11 (embedding into slots). An interval testimony embeds as the pair of propositional slots D ("the effect exceeds δ in the registered direction") and Z ("the effect lies within ±δ"): T_dir ↦ (T, F); F_dir ↦ (F, F); Flat ↦ (F, T); U_width, U_ceiling ↦ (N, N).
Lemma L12 (coherence) and Corollary L13 (the verdict grammars). The embedding matches the exit table on canonical intervals, and the program-bet grammars are then ordinary algebra: a conjunction bet's supported is ∧ₜ over the embedded D slots (F-dominant, so one opposite-direction court refutes); a width- or ceiling-censored court is an N leaf, and the conjunction blocks by exactly the mechanism of Theorem 6.7's gate behavior. Flat is a definite verdict (an F on D and a T on Z), never a gap, which is the "powered, not established" grammar note in algebraic form. [V5: embedding rows; P3 supported and partial rows] ∎

4. Process axioms — commitment and the restless corners

Axiom C1 (commitment). Every parameter a verdict evaluates (thresholds, floors, δ, exits, composition rule) has a commit index strictly earlier than the first observation index of the data the verdict reads. A pre-observation supersession of parameters (the registered-delta) preserves C1; a post-observation one violates it.
Axioms D1, D2 (the restless corners). D1: a slot at corner N whose scope is open incurs the obligation that a docket (a named future court) exist within k steps, or the scope close. D2: a slot at corner B incurs the obligation that a fold (a successor resolving the contest) occur within k steps. The two non-classical corners are thereby obligated states, not resting states — the ledger laws "live requires a docket" and "contested must resolve," stated on the algebra.
Theorem L14 (consistency), Proposition L15 (independence), Proposition L16 (conservativity). {C1, D1, D2} is consistent: a finite trace model satisfying all three exists (exhibited). The axioms are independent: for each, a trace violating it alone is constructible. And the axioms are conservative over the equational theory: they constrain event traces, not testimonies; no equation between testimonies is added or removed, so every model of Definitions 2.4–2.16 extends to a model of the axioms by choosing compliant traces.
Proof. Consistency and independence by the exhibited traces, machine-checked. Conservativity: the trace machine is parametric over the algebra: its states carry corners read from testimonies by Def 2.6 and never write back; the operations of §2 of the calculus are not mentioned by C1/D1/D2, whose vocabulary is commit indices, scopes, dockets, and folds. A derivation of a testimony equation from the axioms would have to use that vocabulary vacuously, hence already be derivable without them. [V6: consistency, independence; V7: C1 trace and violation detection] ∎

5. Reflexive grading and the agreement discount

Definition L17 (meta-testimony). System artifacts (compaction summaries, goldens, memories) are testimonies on slots about the system itself, their lineage tokens the artifacts' provenance. A summary is thereby unverified testimony by construction; a golden's grade renders from its provenance token under ν̂ (hand-derived above computed-then-pinned above recorded); discrediting an artifact is token withdrawal t ∖ l (Def 2.16), which collapses joint derivations through it while independent support survives.
Axiom E3 (agreement discount, at the render). For corroboration-class slots (claims whose support is agreement among sources), the gate's grade is invariant under duplication of same-lineage support: the render deduplicates per lineage before ν̂. E3 cannot be a semiring homomorphism (deduplication breaks additivity), so it lives where Def 2.15 already places discretion (the choice of render), and ℕ[X] already carries the lineage structure it needs; the algebra's own comment ("NOT Set[Lineage]") cuts the other way here on purpose: multiplicity is kept in the carrier and discounted at the gate, so a non-idempotent trust model remains expressible elsewhere. Consistency is definitional. This is the fallible-oracle finding (correlated reviewers shared four of five errors) as an axiom.
